The need · 1 of 2

Attacks scaled by agentic AI

Generative AI is rapidly reshaping the cybersecurity landscape. Attackers can increasingly use the same models as defenders to discover and exploit vulnerabilities — in code, and in agentic systems.

The shift

Frontier models can scale reconnaissance, vulnerability discovery and exploit generation — at attacker speed.

The gap

Swedish healthcare, public administration and software-intensive industry lack repeatable methods for AI-driven security testing today.

The need · 2 of 2

Agentic adoption brings novel vulnerabilities

In Healthcare applications, agents combine language models, sensitive personal data, and tool use — creating new risks: prompt injection, unauthorized data access, and tool misuse.

SAFE-CARE addresses this through controlled environments where new AI models can be safely tested, compared, and used as defensive "microscopes" — under Swedish requirements for privacy, digital sovereignty, and operational safety.

"The modernization of our healthcare system relies on the rapid adoption of AI, but this cannot happen without guaranteed security. We must leverage AI itself to identify these security holes before malicious actors are armed with the same capabilities."

— Magnus Kjellberg, VGR

Research plan · Phase 3, part C

Security as engineered asymmetry

A system is secure enough when it costs an attacker more than their goal is worth. Now that both attack and defense can run as AI agents, that cost is measurable — in tokens, compute, and time.

cost to attack÷cost to defend

Both tracks measure the same thing — goal, cost, success. Once a track is set up, we can search automatically for the strongest defensive configuration, or the cheapest attack path, instead of tuning it by hand.
